About me

I am a sixth-year Computer Science Ph.D. candidate at Simon Fraser University (SFU), where I also serve as a sessional instructor and Research Assistant in the GrUVi ({graphics} ∪ {vision}) Laboratory. I am advised by Dr. Eugene Fiume, and my research focuses on physically and biomechanically based facial animation. Prior to joining SFU, I earned my Master of Engineering in Electrical and Computer Engineering from Chongqing University in 2020, under the supervision of Dr. Junjie Tang. I am originally from Yichun, Jiangxi Province, China.

Outside of academia, I am an active musician, playing both the piano and clarinet. I used to perform with the SFU Concert Orchestra and the SFU Choir (Tenor 2). I also enjoy staying active through tennis, cycling, and gym, and I love spending my downtime reading classic literature and novels. I am a native Mandarin speaker and fluent in English. In professional and public contexts, I prefer to go by "Richard," though I warmly welcome "Zhiyun" from friends and fellow countrymen.

Promising Research Directions & Open Position Descriptions:

1. In-House Physical Soft-Tissue Simulator (FEM / XPBD Solver Core)

Focus: Engineering an independent, high-performance soft-tissue and biomechanical physical simulator tailored for multi-layered facial anatomy. Formulating stable numerical solvers (XPBD/PBD, Projective Dynamics, non-linear FEM) incorporating hyperelastic constitutive strain energy models, volume preservation, passive inertia, multi-tissue coupling (deep actuated muscles to epidermal skin through fascia/fat), and self-collision response.

Ideal for: Students with strong C++ or Python skills, interest in numerical methods, continuum mechanics, linear algebra, or physics engine development. [Learn more →]

Modern AI-Assisted Engineering Workflow:

  • AI & LLMs Encouraged: We actively encourage the use of State-of-the-Art LLMs (e.g., Claude, GPT-4, Gemini) to accelerate algorithm derivation, translate mathematical models into functional code, and expedite debugging.
  • Open-Source Foundations: Open-source solver and graphics architectures serve as solid references that we adapt and extend to meet specialized biomechanical demands.

What We Offer:

  • Financial Support: Funded Research Assistantship (RA) / Developer stipends available for qualified positions.
  • Mentorship: Research mentorship and hands-on guidance in computer graphics, vision, and biomechanics.
  • Career & Academic Impact: Clear opportunities for co-authorship on top-tier publications (e.g., ACM SIGGRAPH / SCA), high-visibility portfolio code, and strong recommendation letters for graduate school or industry R&D.

2. Validating Physically Based Facial Animation via Biological Ground Truth (MRI & sEMG)

Focus: Proving that simulated muscle trajectories reflect true human biology rather than merely visual plausibility. Quantitatively benchmarking simulated muscle activations and landmark displacement paths against open-access dynamic magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) and surface electromyography (sEMG) datasets. Developing automated Python error-metric pipelines and designing in-vivo experimental data collection protocols.

Ideal for: Students interested in biomedical data analysis, signal processing, Python scientific computing (NumPy/SciPy), and empirical validation methods. [Learn more →]

3. Synthesizing Fine-Grained Facial Dynamics: Micro-Expressions & Indigenous Phonemes

Focus: Pushing the boundaries of facial animation to model fine-grained involuntary micro-expressions (e.g., repressed smiles, fleeting brow furrows) via asymmetric, high-frequency, low-duration muscle bursts. Concurrently modeling unique phonetic inventories of Canadian Indigenous languages (such as Coast Salish / Hul’q’umi’num’) requiring distinct lip-rounding and ejective facial kinematics.

Ideal for: Students interested in computer animation, speech acoustics, linguistics, affective computing, and facial landmark tracking. [Learn more →]

4. Automated 3D Facial Asset Generation & Vision Pipelines

Focus: Fully automating the construction of personalized 3D facial assets before simulation. Investigating lightweight monocular smartphone video and landmark tracking (MediaPipe) to infer personalized skull dimensions and rest muscle anchor points, as well as automating boundary generation directly from volumetric scans.

Ideal for: Students with computer vision, geometric processing (Open3D, Trimesh), 3D modeling, and automated pipeline experience. [Learn more →]

5. Interactive Web Simulation & LLM-Driven Physical Modeling (IdeaVibes Competition)

Focus: Engineering high-performance real-time web demonstrations (Interactive Prototype) using WebGL, Three.js, and Web Workers. Smartly querying State-of-the-Art LLMs to accelerate solver development and feature mounting for the IdeaVibes AI competition (collaborating with Prof. Jiangchuan Liu).

Ideal for: Full-stack or frontend developers (Three.js, WebGL, JavaScript/TypeScript), AI prompt engineers, and interactive UI designers. [Learn more →]

6. Automated Pipeline for Skin-to-Muscle Non-Rigid Registration

Focus: Developing automated programmatic tools using Non-Rigid Iterative Closest Point (ICP) and Laplacian surface editing to shrink-wrap and align epidermal skin meshes over customized internal musculature for the entire head, eliminating hours of manual artist setup.

Ideal for: Students with geometry processing, mesh deformation, Python/C++, and 3D graphics experience. [Learn more →]

7. Learning-Based Acceleration for Piano-Style Interface (Neural Surrogates)

Focus: Formulating deep learning surrogate models and physics-informed neural network (PINN) architectures to approximate non-linear soft-tissue dynamics for our interactive piano-style performance interface. Achieving ultra-low-latency (< 2ms) 60 FPS real-time facial expression synthesis while rigorously enforcing volume preservation and physical plausibility.

Ideal for: Students and researchers with machine learning, neural networks (PyTorch), scientific computing, and interactive computer graphics experience. [Learn more →]
